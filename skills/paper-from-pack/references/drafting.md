# Drafting rules

Voice: first person singular for a sole author, plain and concrete, the way an engineer explains something to a peer. Short sentences, concrete nouns, the author's own words from the interview where they exist. No marketing words ("novel", "revolutionary", "state-of-the-art"), no claim of being first.

Content
- Follow the pack's suggested structure and angle. Respect "Must not", "Owns" and "May cite" literally.
- Lead with the fact and its source; then the point.
- Report numbers as the data file does, with n. Keep all limitations the data file states, in the paper, not only in a footnote.
- Separate what was measured from what is argued. A position is labelled as the author's position.
- Do not describe agent work as the author's work. The pack states the AI-use facts; use them in the required statement.
- Client or employer confidentiality as the pack says (anonymised, no team-specific numbers, no names). When unsure, leave it out and ask in OPEN.md.
- Conflicts of interest: if one of the measured tools is the author's, say so in the paper in the neutral form the blind rules allow ("one of the five tools was built by the author") and in SUBMIT.md.
- Prior work: only pack-named works; for each, say how this piece differs.
- No filler openers or closers, no summary paragraphs that repeat the section, no future-work list beyond what the pack supports.

When the data cannot support the requested claim: write the claim the data supports, mark the gap `TO VERIFY: <what would be needed>`, list it in OPEN.md with a concrete collection task. Do not add a hedge to a claim you cannot source; remove it.

When the structure will not fit the limit: cut in this order, recording each cut in OPEN.md: (1) related-work detail that is not needed to place the claim, (2) second examples, (3) secondary table rows (keep the rows the claims use), (4) background. Never shrink fonts, margins, spacing, figure labels. Never delete a stated limitation.
